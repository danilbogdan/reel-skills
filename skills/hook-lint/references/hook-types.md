# Hook types

Six shapes that open short videos well. Pick one per video, then write it in your own words.

| Type | Shape | Example (neutral) | Needs to be true |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal stake | "I kept ... until ..." | "I kept losing my keys until I tried this." | A real story you can tell in 20 s |
| Verdict or news | "[Name] changed [thing]" | "The new phone update changes how you film." | Something actually changed |
| Result plus time | "[Result] in [time]" | "A clean kitchen in 15 minutes." | You can show the process |
| Question | A question the viewer answers in their head | "How long does your commute really take?" | The video answers it |
| Cause | "It's not X. It's Y." | "It's not your skin. It's your pillowcase." | You can explain Y |
| Counted list | "N things that ..." | "5 settings that fix blurry phone video." | Item N is worth the wait |

## Rules of thumb

- The first 3 seconds must contain the promise or the problem. No greeting, no channel intro.
- Caption on screen: 3 to 8 words. Spoken: one sentence, about 4 seconds.
- One claim per hook. Two claims split the attention.
- Use a number only if the video shows where it comes from.
- Everything the hook promises has to be delivered inside the video. A broken promise costs more than a weak hook.

## Test before you shoot

1. Say the hook aloud. Does it sound like you, or like an announcer?
2. Show the caption alone to one person from your audience. What do they think the video is about?
3. Make two versions of the first sentence and run them as separate trial posts, changing one thing only.
